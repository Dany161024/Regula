How does it know the real size?

A web page can't know how big your pixels are — the ruler marks on this site are just decoration. A desktop app can ask the display itself.

  1. 1

    Your display reports its width

    Every monitor tells the computer its physical size. A 14″ ThinkPad says: 309 mm.

  2. 2

    Pixels per millimetre

    1920 px across 309 mm is 6.21 px per mm. Every tick, circle and protractor is drawn with that number.

  3. 3

    Calibrate with any card

    Want it perfect? Hold a bank card, ID or student card (all 85.60 mm wide) to the screen and match the outline. Done once per monitor.

Most displays report their size to the nearest millimetre, which is usually accurate to well under 1 %. Some monitors report nonsense — that's what calibration is for. With two monitors, each one gets its own scale, and when you drag the window across, your measurements stay in millimetres.

Made for exact numbers

Dragging with a mouse is never perfect, so Regula gives you three ways around it.

Snap to a step

Points snap to 0.5, 1 or 5 mm (1/32–1/4″ for inches, 0.5–5° on the protractor). 20 × 50 mm is exactly 20 × 50 mm.

Just type it

Start typing and fields pop up: 20 Enter 50 Enter. Works for rulers, the diagonal, paths, angles and circles.

Nudge by 0.1 mm

Arrow keys move the active point by a tenth of a millimetre — hold Shift for 1 mm.

Four backgrounds

Light, dark, cutting-mat green, and transparent — for measuring things that are on your screen.

Window or fullscreen

Fullscreen by default; F11 (⌃⌘F on a Mac) turns it into a resizable window that remembers its size.

Two monitors? Fine.

Each display keeps its own scale and calibration. Move the window and a 20 mm line is still 20 mm.

Install on Linux

You need Python 3.9+ and PySide6 (Qt 6). Developed and tested on Linux Mint 22 and Fedora 44 with X11; Wayland is untested. On a Mac or a Windows PC, use the native app instead.

  1. Install PySide6

    Any distro, in its own environment:

    python3 -m venv ~/.venvs/regula
~/.venvs/regula/bin/pip install PySide6

    On Ubuntu or Linux Mint you may need sudo apt install python3-venv first. Or use your distro's package: sudo dnf install python3-pyside6 (Fedora), sudo pacman -S pyside6 (Arch) — then run Regula with plain python3.

  2. Download and unpack

    Get regula.zip, then:

    unzip ~/Downloads/regula.zip -d ~/.local/share/

  3. Run it

    ~/.venvs/regula/bin/python ~/.local/share/regula/regula.py

    First time? Press K and calibrate with a card if you need exact results.

  4. Add it to your app menu optional

    mkdir -p ~/.local/share/applications
cat > ~/.local/share/applications/regula.desktop <<EOF
[Desktop Entry]
Type=Application
Name=Regula
Comment=Real-size rulers, protractors and measuring tools
Exec=$HOME/.venvs/regula/bin/python $HOME/.local/share/regula/regula.py
Icon=$HOME/.local/share/regula/regula.svg
Categories=Utility;
StartupWMClass=Regula
Terminal=false
EOF

Settings and calibration are stored in ~/.config/regula/config.json.
